        How it works

        One rule set, every level

        A holon is a whole that is also a part. Motes are the smallest. Cells are made of motes, Bodies of cells, Colonies of bodies, Biomes of colonies. All of them run the same code and climb the same six rungs.

        1. Embody. Whatever level the aperture resolves is the raw capacity: one body that harvests energy as a unit. Everything inside it is finished history. Motes absorb what is under them, higher holons collect through their membrane.
        2. Externalise. It spends energy to deposit works into the world. Works capture extra energy and do part of the harvesting, for whoever stands on them.
        3. Coordinate. Peers bond through shared works and synchronise their phase. Coordinated builders get more structure per unit of energy and use it more fully.
        4. Crisis. Flat consensus among n peers costs each of them (n−1)β. The benefit saturates. Past a certain size the coalition spends more than a quarter of its income agreeing with itself.
        5. Transcend. A coalition in crisis either fragments or closes a membrane. The membrane replaces n² channels with one hub, shelters what is inside, and taxes members so nobody can free-ride.
        6. Recurse. The new holon is a single body again. It starts at rung I, one level up.

        None of this is scheduled. Each rung is measured from what the population is doing, and each can be switched off under Rungs allowed to see what stalls.

        Where the world comes from

        Each world begins before geometry. 320 elements with no properties and no relations are indistinguishable: any one could be swapped for any other. One is marked off, and nothing explains which. Relations then form under two constraints and two rewrite rules. Composing rule applications gives a history. Which application needed which gives a causal order and a clock. Shortest relation paths give distance, and counting how balls grow gives a dimension, which comes out close to 2.

        That is as far as the derivation goes. Laying the sheet onto a field and calling it space is an assumption. So is the energy field and its rules. The simulator marks both as assumed, and The stack shows them next to the open questions in the real chain.

        Persistence cost

        Nothing exists for free. Per tick a mote pays

        c = P·(m₀·sd + mc·capacity) + mv·motility + bonds + k·(n−1)β + deposits

        where d is how many membranes enclose it and s < 1 is the shelter each one gives. A membrane costs its holon cm·√mass + ch·mass, so upkeep per member falls as the body grows. Works decay at a rate set by Entropy, slower for the works of higher levels. A mote that cannot pay dies and its matter returns to the field.

        Possibility and its opposite

        The run starts as a featureless field. Motes condense at random places with uniformly random genomes. That state is the maximum-entropy one: it carries no information, so it cannot be told apart from any other draw of the same noise.

        Order is defined as distance from that state. Let p be the share of all motes in each block of a grid and u the uniform share. The relative entropy

        D(p‖u) = Σ pᵢ log₂(pᵢ / uᵢ)

        is the rate at which an observer accumulates evidence that what they see is not noise: after N samples the chance of mistaking it for noise falls as 2−N·D (Chernoff–Stein). The statistic G = 2N·D·ln 2 has a known distribution under the Poisson null, so its expected value from sampling alone is subtracted. What remains is the order an observer can actually distinguish, with a z-score. Bars drawn hollow are within 3σ of noise.

        Why the aperture matters

        Relative entropy obeys a chain rule under coarse-graining. Halving the resolution splits it exactly:

        D(fine) = D(coarse) + ⟨D(fine | coarse)⟩

        Repeating this from 10-unit blocks up to 640-unit blocks gives seven terms that add up to the total, one per octave of scale. That is the order spectrum. Stopping the aperture down (zooming out) does not lose the order of the higher levels. It is the only place that order can be seen. Negative bars mean motes are spaced more evenly than chance, which is the signature of territory and packing.

        The Genome panel applies the same measure to trait space: bits per mote by which selection has moved the population away from the uniform genome it was born with.

        • Knock out membranes and check whether coordination alone can hold order at large scales.
